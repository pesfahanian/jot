1.2 Borders — re-derived for floating panels
weightlightdarkseparates
edge #B4B8BC #2A2A2E One discrete object from what is outside it. Each floating panel's own full outline — sidebar, editor, status bar, all four sides — plus cards and the dark popover. The window-boundary use moves to tray-line.
hairline #CCD0D3 #1F1F22 Two zones inside one panel. Tab-bar underline, sidebar-header underline, tab separators, control outlines. The workhorse. Sidebar/editor divider — retired, nothing is shared any more. Status-bar top — absorbed into the status bar's own edge. The token stays.
rule #E6E8EA #161618 Divisions inside one zone. Gutter edge, popover rows, status-bar cells. Unchanged by this round.
tray-line new #CCD0D3 = hairline #1F1F22 = hairline The app from the browser. Drawn once, on the tray (§1.2b). A role of its own that borrows hairline's value: deliberately one step quieter than the edge on the panels it holds, so the panels read as the objects and the tray reads as the plane they sit on.
Each theme's lines run toward its own extreme. Light lines are always darker than the ground they divide — rule #E6E8EA (L .93) against surface #FBFCFD (L .99). Dark lines are always lighter — rule #161618 (L .201) against canvas #09090B (L .141) at the gutter and panel #111113 (L .179) in the status bar. A line is never the other direction from its ground in either theme.
What differs is how much room the three weights get. Light has almost a full lightness point between edge, hairline and rule; dark has roughly a tenth of that, because everything is compressed into the bottom of the range. The dark correction — #1C1C1F → #161618 — was about recovering separation from the workhorse (3 lightness points apart read as one border), not about crossing below any surface. Implementing on dark means checking the gap between the three weights, not their direction against the ground.
Why the logic still holds. "Pick by what the line separates" assumed lines exist because two regions touch. In the floating shell nothing touches, so separation between panels is no longer a line at all — it is the seam, a strip of canvas. Lines now only do two jobs: outline an object (edge, tray-line) or divide inside one (hairline, rule). The four existing weights cover both without a new value.
One ground-dependent value. The dark gutter rule now sits on document (#141416, L .192), not canvas; rule #161618 (L .201) is still lighter, as the direction rule requires, but only .009 apart and invisible. On the document ground only, dark rule draws at #1F1F22 — as 8a rendered. The role is unchanged; the gutter is still a rule. Light is unaffected (rule on surface keeps its full separation).
Rule for new work: pick by what the line separates, never by how heavy it should look. A fourth value, float (light #5F6469, dark #2A2A2E), belongs only to things floating above the app — context menus, the filter box — and is what replaces a drop shadow. The tray is not elevated and does not use it.
1.2b Layout layers — tray, seam, panels new
Three layers, back to front. The tray fills the browser viewport. Panels sit inside it. The seam is the canvas left showing between and around them. Names chosen to stay clear of float (elevated things), gutter (line numbers) and frame (what this document calls a render).

1.4b Content-marking wash — --mark

Used for Editor text selection and search-match highlighting — the sidebar search snippets (2b) and any in-document match. A ground behind full-strength ink, never an underline and never a border.
Why 102 A true neutral yellow: 52° clear of the dark accent (50), 27° of the light accent (75), and at a third of their chroma, so it reads as paper marked with a highlighter rather than a tinted accent. It replaces the retired accent-wash in light and the accent-hue #452C10 in dark.
Neighbours The gold tag (95) and the punctuation mark (88–90) share its hue family but never its shape: tags are 8px squares, punctuation is a wavy underline, --mark is only ever a ground behind text. Ink stays at full strength on it — 13:1 in light, 11:1 in dark.

1.9 Spacing, sizing, radius, elevation
Base unit A 2px step, not an 8pt grid. Small increments 4/5/6, pads 8/10/12/14. Sidebar rows 5–6px vertical and 10–12px horizontal; control groups gap 5–6px.
Bar heights Tab bar and sidebar header 38px — they must still match each other across the seam, so the two header underlines read as one line broken by a gap. Status bar 26px content, 28 with its edge. Review pane headers 30px; review toolbar 34px. Identical in both themes.
Sidebar width Default 248px, drag range 180–460px. The handle is the seam itself: an 8px hit target with nothing drawn at rest. The old 1px hairline handle is retired with the divider.
Editor gutter 46px, right-aligned, 10px inner pad, text 20px past the rule. Split pane: 40 / 8 / 16.
Controls Icon buttons 24×20, glyphs 8–11px at 1.5px stroke. Labeled controls 22px, 7px side pad. Menu and bubble actions 24px. Identical in both themes.
Marks Tag square 8px in a list row, 7px on a tab, 11px in the filter box, 14px in the assign menu (inside a 20px ring slot) — the first three at 2px radius, the last two at 4px, so all five read as the same rounded square. Status dots 5px at 2px radius. Focus and selection markers always 2px; proofing underlines always 1.5px.
Fixed slots Sidebar rows reserve an 8px color slot and a 7px pin slot whether filled or not, so names start on one axis. The review control holds one width across all four states so the status bar never reflows.
Radius — both themes 4 · badges, swatches ≥11px6 · buttons, inputs, icon buttons6 6 0 0 · tabs9999 · true pills12 · cards, panels, popovers, sidebar + editor20 · tray only, derivedTabs take the button value on the top two corners only — the bottom edge continues into the document below the active tab, and rounding it would notch where tab meets page. Small tag marks (5–9px) clamp to 2px: at 8px a 4px radius is a circle, which would collide with the pin's outlined circle one slot over and make the same token read as a circle in the sidebar and a rounded square in the assign menu. Radius has to be read as a proportion, not a constant. The pin head and magnifier lens are the only true circles in either theme.
Radius — provenance 4 · badges, tags 6 · buttons, inputs 6 6 0 0 · tabs 9999 · true pills 12 · cards, panels Tabs take the button value on the top two corners only — the bottom edge continues into the document below the active tab, and rounding it would notch where tab meets page.
Elevation No drop shadows and no glow anywhere, either theme. Ordinary elevation is a border weight plus a luminance step: light lifts to surface with a float border; dark steps panel → elevated. One signature exception, both themes now, reserved for the AI review panel and the shortcut keys inside it. Dark: inset 0 1px 0 #2D2E30, 0 0 0 1px #242426, inset 0 -3px 0 #0A0A0C. Light: inset 0 1px 0 #FFFFFF, 0 0 0 1px oklch(.90 .005 250), inset 0 -3px 0 oklch(.855 .006 250). A 1px top highlight and a dark bottom inset in both — how a physical key catches a ceiling light. Not reused elsewhere. The light version requires the panel ground (#E4E6E9), not surface: a near-white key has no room above itself for a highlight. That ground is part of the token, not a suggestion.
One shape language, two palettes
Every dimension above is now shared: sidebar 248, tab bar 38, status bar 26, gutter 46, icon buttons 24×20, seam 8, tray radius 20, and the same four radii. The floating-panel structure is a shape decision and is identical in both themes (8a dark, 8b light). The earlier drift (dark ran 2px larger on five dimensions) had no reason behind it — it came from the dark brief being written as its own round. Light's values won because the smaller chrome gives the document more room, which is the whole point of the app.
Two differences remain, both deliberate: dark's line-height (1.7 vs 1.65) and tighter wordmark tracking (−0.025em vs −0.01em), compensating for light type reading heavier against a dark ground. Everything else that differs between the themes is a color.
1.10 Three confirmations
Dark spelling color It is dark's own --destructive, oklch(.64 .19 22) / #F0434E — not light's #B32035. The §1.6 cell reading "reuses light value" was wrong and is corrected below. What carries across themes is the token identity: spelling and app-failure share one variable, because a misspelling and a failed request make the same claim. The value behind that variable is per-theme — #B32035 at L .50 would nearly vanish on a near-black ground.
Accent vs clay, dark clay .09/25 accent .16/50 clay-tagged file review style Holds, and for a different reason than gold did. 25° of hue is a narrower gap than the 45° to gold, but clay and the accent separate on both axes at once — clay is pinker and much flatter, the accent oranger and nearly twice the chroma. The gold problem was that hue agreed and only chroma disagreed; here neither agrees. The 8px-dot test above is the one that matters, and at that size the accent dot reads as a different material, not a brighter version of the same one. No change needed.
Keycap on light It works, with one constraint that is now part of the token: the panel must sit on panel #E4E6E9, never surface #FBFCFD. On panel there is .075 of lightness left to white for the top highlight — close to the .09 dark has above its elevated surface. On surface there is .01 and the effect dies. Rendered in 3g. Honest caveat: the small keys read better than the large panel, where the 12px radius makes the bevel easy to miss. If it has to earn its place on one element only, it should be the keys.
